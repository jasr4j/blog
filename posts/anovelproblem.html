A Novel Problem: Redeeming the AI-fication of a knowledge based field (Mathematics)

November 25, 2026

Disclaimer: I am not the most knowledgable when it comes to AI or math, but I am curious and hope to learn something new from any responses.

AI is now a very powerful tool, and its purpose of building mathematical proofs using existing knowledge, has recently been brought back to the forefront of AI frontier labs' innovation. It is possible to solve complex problems with the power of AI. But there becomes an issue. Since AI is artificial, as the name implies, meaning it is trained on existing data, there is a bound to what AI knows.

We need to create a mathematical problem that AI doesn't have the means to solve. Math as a field is being redefined everyday and the way to change it for the better isn't simply to explore maths that applies to the real world, but explore maths beyond our understanding of life. Beyond geometry in dimensions, we could analyze the geometry of gradients that predict a gradient. This is a simple example that has already been done. In order to pose such a problem, I will also attempt to solve it (possibly more in a future post).

So far, my ideas include graph theory combining with non euclidean computational geometry. In some way, we could use interesting geometry to build unknown graphs. I am bouncing off older ideas like group theory which existed with no purpose. Hopefully I can creatively define something that can stump AI and offer opportunity for renewed interests for humans in mathematical research, with our creative brains that we actually have (not GPUs).

This problem may or may not change maths, but slowly, maths is becoming a subject where creativity triumphs problem solving and new subfields have the potential to be discovered. This is where AI can use a small understanding to bridge gaps. In most AI classes, one learns about uncertainty, but also about knowledge. Creativity is like a seed for a knowledge base, and simply put, using uncertainty, we can predict what the next thing will be based on our expanding knowns and unknowns and how likely something is to occur.

This may also pose issues to AI, where the subfield is so novel that existing neurons making connections will only hurt the AI's training. This is all hypothetical, but I would be glad to reply to any emails on the subject. This is not meant to hurt any sentiments, just posing a question about the future of mathematics and AI.
